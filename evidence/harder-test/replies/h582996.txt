**1. Larchmere Housing Trust, annual report proofread: APPLY**
Reason: £240 for about 10 hours comes to £24 an hour, which is above your minimum. It's the same kind of work you already do for the charity. 10 hours over three weeks is about 3 to 4 hours a week, so it fits.
Next step: Apply and mention your charity report and bid proofreading. Before you agree the fee, ask whether you'll be proofreading a Word file or the print-ready PDF, and how many rounds of corrections £240 covers. A fixed fee falls below £22 an hour if the job runs past about 11 hours.

**2. Seaview Dental, blog posts: SKIP**
Reason: £30 a post at their estimate of 1.5 hours is £20 an hour, which is below your minimum. That's also their best case. Health content for patients takes research, and dental clinics usually want SEO, which you haven't done. Realistically you'd earn less than £20.
Next step: Don't apply at £30. If you want to try, ask for at least £35 a post and see if they agree. If they don't, leave it.

**3. Brightpath Learning, content writer: SKIP (as posted)**
Reason: £35 an hour is good, but the unpaid 1,000-word sample is five times your 200-word limit.
Next step: Send one short message offering two or three of your published newsletters, or a sample of up to 200 words, in place of the 1,000-word one. If they still insist on the full unpaid sample, walk away.

**4. Quillhub, newsletter editing: SKIP**
Reason: £24 minus Quillhub's 20% fee leaves £19.20 an hour, which is below your minimum. The work itself suits you well, and 6 hours a week would fit.
Next step: To take home £22 after the fee, you'd need £27.50 an hour before it. If the platform lets you, ask the client for that. Otherwise skip it.

**5. Northdale Clinic, patient leaflets: APPLY**
Reason: £40 an hour for about 12 hours is well above your minimum. You'd be rewriting the leaflets in plain English, which is close to what you already do. The medical risk is lower than it looks: they say medical experience is "preferred but not essential", and a clinician checks every leaflet.
Next step: Apply and be honest that you haven't done medical writing. Lead with plain-English editing and writing for the public, and send a short before-and-after edit if you have one. Ask when they need it done. At 8 hours a week, 12 hours takes about a week and a half.

**If you get both 1 and 5**
Together they come to about 22 hours. That fits if they overlap by only a week or so. Agree the deadlines before you accept both.

**Places to look for more work like 1 and 5**
- Charity sector job boards that list freelance and contract roles. Your charity background is your strongest selling point there.
- Contact charities, housing associations and small nonprofits directly. Annual reports and funding bids come round every year, so one client can bring repeat work.
- The Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading (CIEP). It's the UK professional body for proofreaders, and its training and directory help clients find you. Membership costs money, so check the current fees before joining.
- LinkedIn. Make "charity newsletter and proofreading" clear in your headline, and tell your current charity contacts that you have capacity.
- General platforms such as Upwork and PeoplePerHour. They're fine, but take any platform fee off the rate before you compare it with your £22, as with Quillhub.
