**1. Reels from supplied footage, Ironbark Climbing** (https://jobs.example/v/3301)
Pay: £320 for eight reels
**APPLY**
Why: This is the same work you've done for two years: short reels in Premiere Pro from footage someone else shot. If their 12-hour estimate is right, it pays about £26.67 an hour. Over three weeks that's about 4 hours a week, which fits easily. The estimate is theirs, not yours, though. If the job takes more than about 12.8 hours, you drop below £25 an hour.
Check next: how much raw footage there is, how long each reel should be, whether captions or music are included, how many rounds of changes they expect, and when they pay.

**2. Weekly reels, The Copper Kettle cafe** (https://jobs.example/v/3325)
Pay: £100 a week
**MAYBE**
Why: Short reels from phone footage are well within what you can do. Whether the pay works depends on the hours. At 3 hours it's about £33 an hour, at 4 hours exactly £25, and at 5 hours £20, which is below your minimum. Three or four reels in four hours a week is tight.
Check next: three or four reels each week, and which? How many changes do they want? Who writes captions and picks music? How long does the arrangement run, and when do they pay? If it looks like 5 hours, ask for more money or for fewer reels.

**3. Social video editor, Ancoats Agency** (https://jobs.example/v/3346)
Pay: £27 an hour, 10 hours a week
**MAYBE**
Why: The rate is above your minimum and the work matches your experience. But 10 hours spread over three office days in Manchester means travel you won't be paid for, which cuts your real hourly rate. Agencies also often want motion graphics, and the advert doesn't say which software they use.
Check next: how the 10 hours fall across the three office days, which software they use, whether they expect After Effects or motion graphics, and whether this is freelance or closer to a part-time job.
