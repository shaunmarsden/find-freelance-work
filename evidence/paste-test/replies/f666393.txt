**1. Sales spreadsheet tidy-up, Owl & Ivy Gifts** (https://gigs.example/j/7731)
Pay: £150 fixed price
Verdict: **APPLY**
Why: This is the work you've done for five years: tidying spreadsheets and building pivot tables. If it takes their estimate of six hours, that's £25 an hour. If it runs to eight hours you'd still get £18.75, so you have some room. Two weeks at ten hours a week is plenty of time.
Check next: Ask to see the spreadsheet, or a sample of it, before you agree the price. "Messy" can mean anything. Agree what "done" means (which columns, which summaries) and how many rounds of changes are included, so the fixed price doesn't stretch to fifteen hours.

**3. Xero reconciliation, Penarth Community Garden** (https://gigs.example/j/7752)
Pay: not stated ("to be discussed")
Verdict: **MAYBE**
Why: Four hours a month fits easily around your job, and you know Xero. But you've done data entry in Xero, and reconciling accounts is a step beyond that: matching bank lines and sorting out the ones that don't match. Be honest about that difference. Also, a small charity may not pay anything near £18 an hour.
Check next: Ask what reconciling involves for them (just matching bank feeds, or fixing past errors too?) and what rate they have in mind. If it's routine bank-feed matching and they'll pay £18 or more, it could be a good regular job. If they need someone to sort out their books, that's bookkeeper work, and you should say so.
